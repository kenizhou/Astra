# Design: The Evolved Iris texture chapter

## Problem Statement

The Astra Mail UI reads primitive and unprofessional: flat dark panes, a
uniform cool surface with no ground/ink relationship, no hairline structure,
and system-stack type with no voice. The Evolved Iris direction is settled
(decision 01bb418e); what is missing is the material layer that makes the
product feel crafted.

## Demand Evidence

Self-demand only, stated honestly: "I am that person (dogfood)" on whether
anyone would be upset if Astra disappeared, and "Not a daily driver yet" on
whether Astra carries real mail today. The irritation is builder's-chair
irritation: felt while reviewing screens, not while living in the product.
Real but unpriced by use.

## Status Quo

The founder builds and reviews the UI (fixture harness, design audits, demo
boards) without daily-driving the product. The workaround for real mail is
another client. The cost of the flatness, today, is confidence in the
product's direction, not hours lost in triage.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

The user is the founder, whose day holds all three mail moments; ranked on
a forced choice: triage first (the morning scan of a full inbox), then
reading, then calendar. The wedge for texture: the reading pane proves the material
first (the low-density debut), and the depth investment lands on the
message list row (the triage surface).

## Constraints

- The Evolved Iris direction is settled; this work extends it, never reopens it.
- The UI1 design-system slice is specced and awaiting its gate; texture must
  land as its chapter, not a competing track.
- The product contract pins offline operation, the fail-closed AI posture,
  and the calm-Operate surface rules; texture must not break any of them.
- One engineer, one machine, one week of attention at most.

## Premises

1. The flatness is a material problem, not an effects problem: warm ground,
   warm ink, hairline structure, and a typographic voice fix "primitive"
   better than shadows, glass, or gradients. (Superhuman's documented
   system is the evidence; Spark's "polished but generic" is the warning.)
2. Texture lands on triage first as the investment target, revised in
   session: the material debuts on the reading pane (the low-density proof
   surface) and the depth investment lands on the triage list.
3. This extends Evolved Iris as its texture chapter; no fourth direction
   opens.
4. One real day of the founder's own mail through Astra accompanies the
   texture work, because builder's-chair irritation needs a usage check.

## Cross-Model Perspective

Second opinion (Claude subagent, cold read): the strongest version is "a
mail client whose craftsmanship is visible at the level of material, so the
morning triage feels like reading a well-set broadsheet." Its most revealing
quote: "Not a daily driver yet": until one real day runs through the
product, texture is an aesthetic opinion; after, a functional hypothesis.
It challenged premise 2 (triage-first): the message list is the riskiest
surface on which to debut a new material, so prove it on the reading pane
first. The founder accepted the revision.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Material token pass (minimal viable)

A thin texture layer in theme.css only: warm ground hierarchy, warm ink,
hairline borders, the type voice. Effort S, risk low. Rejected as
insufficient alone: "professional" lives in the reading pane and the list
row, not in tokens.

### Approach B: The texture chapter in UI1 (ideal architecture)

Material tokens, the reading-pane debut, the hero triage row with its full
state family, harness-injection auditability, the dogfood run as
verification, all folded into the UI1 spec through its gate. Effort L,
risk medium. Kept as the landing vehicle.

### Approach C: The 48-hour harness proof (creative)

Harness-only, no production code: the texture as a fixtures-mode theme
variant on the real components, one real day of the founder's inbox as
fixture data, a real triage run, notes on every moment the texture helps
or lies. Effort S, risk low. Only survivors get specced.

## Recommended Approach

C feeding B: the 48-hour harness proof first (the founder is not
daily-driving; one real day reprices everything), then the texture chapter
lands in UI1 with the dogfood notes as its premise. Mechanics: a
fixtures-mode theme variant (theme.texture.css) on the real components, a
real-day export of the founder's inbox into the fixture format, a recorded
triage session in both themes and densities, and only the survivors written
into the spec.


## Texture Material Values (approved 2026-10-14, plan-design-review D2)

- Dark theme: the neutral series shifts from hue 258 (cool slate) to a
  warm graphite (hue ~60, very low chroma: warm, never brown); the ink
  warms from oklch 0.935 toward a warm off-white; the reading pane gains
  a paper surface one step lighter than the app canvas; a hairline token
  (1px, warm grey, low chroma) replaces border-subtle on pane and row
  separations.
- Light theme: the ground becomes an honest warm parchment instead of
  cool white.
- The type voice: weight stops 380/450/560 (light previews, medium
  senders, semibold subjects), replacing the default 400/500/600 stops;
  the bundled face decision (UI1 D2) carries them.
- This answers Open Question 1: the warm ground gets the dark-specific
  answer (warm graphite), not a hope that warmth survives dark mode.

## Hero Row State Family (approved 2026-10-14, D3)

| State | Rule |
|---|---|
| default | ink sender, muted preview |
| hover | the warm hairline lifts to the hover tint |
| selected | accent-tinted ground patch plus the hairline box |
| unread | semibold sender plus the accent dot (never bold and colour together) |
| unread + selected | both; the dot wins the accent role |
| flagged | the flag glyph in amber; no ground change |
| keyboard focus | the ring token, visible in both themes |

## Dogfood Run Mechanics (approved 2026-10-14, D4)

- The export: a read through the app's own surface (the MCP query layer
  or astra-cli messages --json) mapped into the mailbox_list.json row
  shape, so the fixture harness serves the founder's real inbox.
- The recording: five moments total spanning helped and lied, in
  docs/dogfood/ (the directory the spec already names).


## Texture Execution (approved 2026-10-14, design-shotgun: A3 "Warm, sharper")

The material values, settled by board review against the live-rendered
variants (the founder rated A 5/5 first, asked for more like it, and
picked the sharpened execution over the softer one):

- Dark ground: warm graphite, series hue 60 at very low chroma
  (background oklch 0.205 0.014 60, panel 0.258 0.017 60, paper
  0.255 0.019 60: the reading pane pushes visibly lighter than the
  canvas).
- Hairline: 1px, oklch 0.44 0.018 60 in dark (glanceable structure),
  oklch 0.78 0.022 90 in light, replacing border-subtle on pane and row
  separations.
- Light ground: parchment, background oklch 0.948 0.017 90.
- Ink: warm off-white oklch 0.94 0.009 60 in dark.
- Type stops: 380 previews, 450 senders, 560 subjects; the reading
  pane subject one step prouder at 640 with -0.015em tracking.
- Accent discipline unchanged: the iris primary, the unread badge, and
  the selected row only.
- Reference: the approved render at
  ~/.gstack/projects/kenizhou-Astra/designs/texture-mail-20261014/shots/texture-a3.jpg
  (the harness page client/web/.gstack/designs/texture/texture-a3.html).
- This extends UI1 D2's material section; the texture chapter's tokens
  are these values, not a rediscovery.

## Open Questions

- Answered: the warm ground gets the dark-specific answer (warm
  graphite), recorded in Texture Material Values.
- Does the dogfood run change the reading-pane-first debut order?

## Success Criteria

- The founder completes one real triage of their own inbox in the textured
  harness build and can name five moments, total, spanning where the texture helped and lied.
- The founder's own "not professional" verdict flips on the reading pane
  and the list, in both themes, on the rendered UI.
- Only evidence-backed texture enters the UI1 spec; the spec's gate closes
  with the texture chapter included or explicitly cut.

## Distribution Plan

The existing pipeline covers this: the texture chapter rides the UI1 slice
through the normal contract process (spec, review, gate, squash-merged PR).

## Dependencies

- The fixture harness (landed) and CAND-1 event injection (at the UI1 gate).
- A real-day fixture export of the founder's inbox (new, small tooling:
  an MCP or CLI read into the fixture JSON shape).
- The UI1 spec gate (open; this chapter's evidence feeds it).

## The Assignment

Before any texture token lands: run one real day of your own mail through
Astra (the harness can serve it after a small export), triage it for real,
and write down five moments, total, spanning where the UI helped and where it lied.
That note file is the texture chapter's premise, replacing my landscape
reading with your evidence.

## What I noticed about how you think

- You said "all the 3 moments" and then, pushed once, "1. Triage first."
  You rank when someone makes you. The texture budget needs that ranking
  instinct applied before you spend it, every time.
- You corrected me upward mid-session: "the current design is not
  professional." You sharpened the brief instead of accepting mine. Keep
  doing that to your own releases.
- You answered against your own interest: "Not a daily driver yet" was the
  most useful sentence in the session, and it cost you a premise. That
  honesty is the thing that makes the dogfood run work.

### R2-1: clarity (minor)

**Problem**

> The Recommended Approach requires a recorded triage session 'in both themes and densities', but no density scale or set of densities is ever defined in the document.

**Remedy**

> Name the densities to be exercised (e.g., the density options the list row supports) or drop the term if only the default density is in scope.

### R2-2: clarity (minor)

**Problem**

> Success criterion 2 requires the 'not professional' verdict to flip 'in both themes', while Open Question 1 leaves unresolved whether the warm ground holds in the dark theme at all. The criterion reads as unconditional even though its dark-theme half depends on an open question the harness proof is meant to answer.

**Remedy**

> Condition the both-themes criterion on the outcome of the dark-theme open question (e.g., the verdict flips in both themes using the dark-specific answer if one proves necessary).

### R2-3: clarity (minor)

**Problem**

> The Assignment demands writing down 'the five moments where the UI helped and where it lied', but success criterion 1 only requires that the founder 'can name the moments the texture helped and lied' with no count, so the verification bar and the assignment disagree on whether five moments are required and whether five means total or per category.

**Remedy**

> Align the two passages: state one count (e.g., five moments total spanning helped and lied) in both the Assignment and success criterion 1.

### R2-4: clarity (minor)

**Problem**

> Effort labels S and L are used for Approaches A, B, and C with no definition of the scale, so the reader cannot check them against the stated one-week constraint.

**Remedy**

> Define the effort scale (what S and L mean in days or weeks of one engineer's attention) where the labels first appear.

### R2-5: clarity (minor)

**Problem**

> The Constraints pin 'one week of attention at most', but the Recommended Approach chains the 48-hour proof (C, Effort S) into the texture chapter landing in UI1 (B, Effort L) through an open spec gate, without stating whether the one-week budget covers only the harness proof or also the UI1 chapter work.

**Remedy**

> State the scope of the one-week budget explicitly: whether it bounds only Approach C and the evidence write-up, with Approach B's effort governed by the UI1 slice's own schedule.
